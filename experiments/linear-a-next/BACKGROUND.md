## 1. Find-site gazetteer

## 2. Contemporary foreign sources naming Crete or Cretans

### 2a. Egyptian

**Keftiu (kftiw) in general.**
- Attested from the early 18th Dynasty. The tomb paintings run Hatshepsut–Amenhotep II. Rekhmire's scenes are dated 1462–1455/50, overpainted by 1436 (Haider).
- Egyptian variants: Keft, Keftiu, Kaftu. Akkadian kaptarītum (adj.) and Kaptara; Ugaritic kptr; Hebrew Kaphtor.
- The association with Crete is **[E]** for the 15th century. In the 13th century the label drifts toward the Levant. **[D]** Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keftiu
- Kyriakidis 2002, "Indications on the nature of the language of the Keftiw from Egyptian sources", *Ägypten und Levante* 12. It covers three sources: the writing board, the Kom el-Hetan names and the London Medical Papyrus spells. From the Egyptian renderings it infers stops p, t, k and liquids (r). https://www.researchgate.net/publication/270485625

**Writing board BM EA 5647** (early 18th Dynasty school board). It has a heading "making names of Keftiu" followed by **8 names**.
- Editions: Peet, T. E. 1927, "The Egyptian writing-board B.M. 5647 bearing Keftiu names", in S. Casson (ed.), *Essays in Aegean Archaeology presented to Sir Arthur Evans*. Also Vercoutter 1956; Helck 1979; Kyriakidis 2002. Recent study: "Crossing the wine-dark sea from Knossos to Memphis: Hellenized Cretans in Egypt in the mid-18th Dynasty (BM EA 5647)", ResearchGate 398121968 (not accessible).
- Normalised forms online (unsourced hobby list, https://www.peiraeuspubliclibrary.com/names/europa/minoan.html): **Yishharu (ꜣš-ḥꜣ-rꜣ?), Nashuja (Nꜣšwy/"Nasuy"), Yikashata ("Akashata/Akashou"), Yidini ("Adinai"), Pinaruti ("Pinaruta"), Rusa, Bansabira**. That is 7 of 8. The eighth is not recovered here. **[D]** Egyptological transliterations should be taken from Peet 1927 / Kyriakidis 2002.
- Affinity is disputed. Some names look Semitic or Hurrian (e.g. Ishḫara-like Yishharu; Bansabira possibly Semitic *bin-*), some Anatolian. None is accepted as Minoan. **[D]**
- **No published LA word has been accepted as matching any of these names.** Possible checks: ru-sa*, pi-na-ru-*, a-di-na-*. **[S]**

**London Medical Papyrus BM EA 10059** (c. 1325; Wreszinski 1912). Two spells, nos. 32–33, are "in the language of Keftiu". Others in the same papyrus are Northwest Semitic (Steiner 1992, *JNES* 51).
- One spell is transliterated s3-n-ti-k3-pw-py-w3-y-i-y-m3-n-ti-r-k3-k3-i-r. Zeke's tentative reading is "santi kapawa piyawaya iya mantil kakail".
- The samuna-disease spell: w-b-q-i ... s3-t-t ... s3-b-w-j-7-3-jj-d3-3 ... hw-m-c-k3-3-t-w ... r-t3-jj.
- Source: Zeke (minoablog 2010), http://minoablog.blogspot.com/2010/02/minoan-incantations-on-egyptian-papyri.html **[S]**. He notes k3-pw ~ LA ka-pa/ku-pa, i-j ~ LA i-ja, and s3-b-w- ~ LA su-pu2-*188. All **[S]**, blog-level.
- There are no word dividers, so segmentation is unreliable. **[E]**

**Magic Papyrus Harris (BM EA 10042), XII 1–5.** A spell with foreign words, sometimes counted among Keftiu sources. Its classification is **[D]**.

**Kom el-Hetan "Aegean List"** (statue base En = PWN V). Mortuary temple of Amenhotep III, c. 1390–1352.
- Editions: Edel 1966; Edel & Görg 2005 (ÄAT 50). Re-analysis: Cline & Stannish 2011, *JAEI* 3.2: 6–16, https://egyptianexpedition.org/articles/sailing-the-great-green-sea-amenhotep-iiis-aegean-list-from-kom-el-hetan-once-more/ (PDF read for this brief).

- No personal names of Kaptarites are given in these texts. So there are **no names to match against LA**, only the ethnic kaptarû/kaptarītum.

### 2c. Ugarit (14th–13th centuries)
- Ugaritic **kptr**: in the Baal Cycle, the craftsman god Kothar-wa-Hasis has his throne in kptr and his inherited land in ḥkpt (Memphis) (KTU 1.3 VI 14–16 and parallels **[U]**). **[E]**
- Akkadian at Ugarit, **RS 16.238**: King Ammistamru II exempts the merchant Sinaranu's ship "returning from" KUR.DUGUD.RI. DUGUD may be read kabtu, giving *Kabturi* = Caphtor. **[D]** reading.
- No Cretan personal names.

### 2d. Hebrew Bible (1st millennium, retrospective)
- Kaphtor: Deut 2:23; Jer 47:4; Amos 9:7 (Philistine origin). Kaphtorim: Gen 10:14.
- Kerethim/Cherethites: 1 Sam 30:14; Ezek 25:16; Zeph 2:5.
- Useful only for the name form Kaphtor, with no Bronze Age lexicon. **[E]**
